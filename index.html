Wind Actions to AS/NZS 1170.2:2021 basic calculator

Site wind speed, design wind speed and preliminary design wind pressure for enclosed rectangular buildings.

Scope & disclaimer: This is a preliminary/educational tool, not a substitute for the full Standard or engineering judgement. It covers Sections 2–4 (site & design wind speed) and a simplified Section 5 (enclosed rectangular buildings, walls and roof pitch <10° only). It does not cover: Section 6 dynamic response (Cdyn is left as a manual input, default 1.0), Appendices A–E, area reduction/combination/local pressure/porous cladding factors (Ka, Kc, Kl, Kp are taken as 1.0), NZ lee-zone multipliers, C/D coastal-distance interpolation, or pitched roofs ≥10°. Always verify against AS/NZS 1170.2:2021 and have results checked by a suitably qualified engineer. Full scope notes →

1. Building (plan size, height, roof, orientation)

Describe the building first — its plan dimensions and height are used both for the site-exposure calculation below (average roof height) and for the wall/roof pressure calculation further down.

Drag the compass (or use the dropdown above) to set the building's bearing. θ = 0° is the face used as the origin for the design wind speed table below (Clause 2.3: the governing speed in the sector ±45° either side); the shape at centre reflects the selected building type and, where applicable, the plan dimensions (W × L) entered above. Circular shapes have no orthogonal faces, so the θ axes and ±45° sectors are hidden for those types.

Shielding multiplier, Ms (Clause 4.3, Table 4.2)

Ms is forced to 1.0 automatically for h > 25 m. Calculating the shielding parameter (s) from upwind building geometry (Cl 4.3.3) is not automated here — enter s directly if known, otherwise leave blank for the conservative default (no shielding credit, Ms = 1.0).

Topographic multiplier, Mt (Clause 4.4)

Sourcing H and Lu: draw a line along the wind direction, through the site and the upwind crest, in Google Earth (desktop/Pro) and use its elevation profile / path tool — read H as the rise to the crest and Lu as the distance back from the crest to where the profile drops to half of H. Google Earth's terrain is SRTM-derived (~30 m grid) — fine for a preliminary check, but for a site near a significant hill/escarpment prefer an official DEM/contour source (Geoscience Australia's ELVIS, LINZ data in NZ, or a site survey) for the final design value.

3. Design wind pressure – enclosed rectangular building (Section 5, simplified)

Walls are fully covered (Tables 5.2(A)–(C)). Roofs are only covered for pitch <10° (Table 5.3(A)) — for pitched roofs ≥10°, enter a Cp,e value you've sourced from Tables 5.3(B)/(C) directly. Ka, Kc, Kl, Kp are all taken as 1.0 (no area reduction / local cladding factors) — apply these separately for cladding and fixing design.

Plan size (W × L), roof height, roof pitch and wind direction relative to the ridge are set in the Building section above.
